## 1. Risk Governance Model

**Risk governance** is the framework of practices, roles, responsibilities, and processes that ensure risks to AI-OS governance objectives are identified, assessed, treated, monitored, and reported in a structured, accountable, and transparent manner.

### 1.1 Scope

Risk governance covers risks to AI-OS governance itself, including:

- **Governance process risks**: Failure of governance bodies to function (quorum failure, chair vacancy, process breakdown).
- **Policy integrity risks**: Invalid, conflicted, or inconsistently applied policies.
- **Authority risk**: Unauthorized authority exercise, delegation chain failures, revoked authority misuse.
- **Compliance risks**: Breach of regulatory or standards obligations through the AI-OS system.
- **Auditability risks**: Loss or corruption of audit trails, failure to produce evidence.
- **Data governance risks**: Data integrity, lineage breakdown, unauthorized access.
- **Agent governance risks**: Misbehaving agents, capability escalation, unauthorized capability usage.

### 1.2 Risk Governance Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Proactive Identification** | Risks are identified before they materialize, through continuous monitoring and periodic review. |
| **Structured Assessment** | Risks are assessed using a consistent methodology (likelihood × impact). |
| **Accountable Treatment** | Every risk has a designated owner responsible for treatment. |
| **Transparent Reporting** | Risk posture is reported at defined intervals to governance bodies. |
| **Continuous Monitoring** | Risk status is continuously assessed through event-driven monitoring. |
| **Traceable Decisions** | Every risk-related decision is recorded with full audit trail. |

## 2. Risk Lifecycle

The risk lifecycle manages risks from identification through closure. The lifecycle is managed by G-07 (Risk Manager) and governed by the Risk Committee (under G-04 Governance Council).

```text
Risk Lifecycle:
IDENTIFIED → ASSESSED → TREATMENT_PLANNED → TREATMENT_IN_PROGRESS 
  → MONITORING → CLOSED
              ↘ REOPENED (new trigger event)
```

### 2.1 Risk Lifecycle States

| State | Description | Entry Criteria | Exit Criteria |
|-------|-------------|----------------|---------------|
| **IDENTIFIED** | Risk has been detected and logged. | Risk identification event received. | Assessment completed. |
| **ASSESSED** | Risk has been evaluated (likelihood, impact, score). | Assessment complete. | Treatment plan approved. |
| **TREATMENT_PLANNED** | A treatment strategy has been identified and proposed. | Treatment proposal developed. | Treatment approved. |
| **TREATMENT_IN_PROGRESS** | Treatment actions are being executed. | Approval to implement treatment. | Treatment applied or monitoring established. |
| **MONITORING** | Risk is under continuous observation; treatment is in effect. | Treatment implemented; monitoring active. | Risk mitigated, accepted, or residual risk stable. |
| **CLOSED** | Risk is mitigated/accepted and no longer requires active management. | Risk assessment confirms acceptable residual risk. | None — terminal state, but can reopen. |

### 2.2 Risk Reopening

A closed risk may be **reopened** if new trigger events indicate the risk has materialized or a new risk factor has emerged. Reopening creates a new instance linked to the original risk.

## 3. Risk Register

The **risk register** is the authoritative inventory of all identified risks within the AI-OS governance scope. It is maintained by G-07 and stored as an artifact in G-03.

### 3.1 Risk Register Entry

Each risk register entry is defined by the Risk Schema (see [schemas.md §Risk Schema](./schemas.md)):

| Field | Type | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `riskId` | string (UUID v4) | Unique identifier. |
| `name` | string | Human-readable name. |
| `version` | string | Semantic version. |
| `description` | string | Risk description, causes, potential impacts. |
| `riskType` | string | Type: `operational`, `security`, `compliance`, `strategic`, `financial`, `reputational`. |
| `category` | string | Sub-category within risk type. |
| `likelihood` | string | `rare`, `unlikely`, `possible`, `likely`, `almost_certain`. |
| `impact` | string | `insignificant`, `minor`, `moderate`, `major`, `catastrophic`. |
| `riskScore` | number | Calculated score (1–25). |
| `riskOwner` | string | Entity responsible for managing the risk. |
| `riskOwnerType` | string | `agent`, `authority`, `governance_body`, `role`, `team`. |
| `mitigationStrategies` | array[MitigationStrategy] | Strategies to reduce likelihood or impact. |
| `existingControls` | array[ControlReference] | Controls currently in place. |
| `effectiveFrom` | string (ISO 8601) | When the assessment becomes effective. |
| `effectiveUntil` | string (ISO 8601) or null | When the assessment expires. |

### 3.2 Risk Scoring

Risk score is calculated as:

```text
riskScore = likelihoodIndex × impactIndex
```

Where:
- **likelihoodIndex**: rare=1, unlikely=2, possible=3, likely=4, almost_certain=5
- **impactIndex**: insignificant=1, minor=2, moderate=3, major=4, catastrophic=5

Score ranges map to risk categories:

| Score Range | Risk Level | Response |
|-------------|------------|----------|
| 1–3 | Low | Monitor |
| 4–6 | Medium | Treat, prioritize based on capacity |
| 7–10 | High | Treat, escalate to committee |
| 11–15 | Very High | Treat immediately, escalate to council |
| 16–25 | Critical | Treat immediately, emergency response |

## 4. Risk Assessment

Risk assessment is the process of determining the likelihood and impact of identified risks, and calculating an overall risk score.

### 4.1 Assessment Process

1. **Risk Identification**: Detect and describe the risk (G-07, operating context).
2. **Likelihood Analysis**: Assess the probability of the risk occurring.
3. **Impact Analysis**: Assess the consequences if the risk materializes.
4. **Risk Scoring**: Calculate the risk score (likelihood × impact).
5. **Risk Categorization**: Assign the risk to a category based on score.
6. **Assessment Recording**: Record the assessment in the risk register (G-03).

### 4.2 Assessment Frequency

| Risk Level | Assessment Frequency |
|------------|---------------------|
| Low | Annual review |
| Medium | Semi-annual review |
| High | Quarterly review |
| Very High | Monthly review |
| Critical | Continuous monitoring |

### 4.3 Assessment Authority

Risk assessments must be conducted by or under the authority of:

- The **Risk Committee** (for domain risks).
- The **Security Council** (for security risks).
- The **Governance Council** (for strategic risks).
- **Designated risk owners** (for operational risks within their scope).

## 5. Risk Treatment

Risk treatment is the process of selecting and implementing measures to modify risk — reducing likelihood, reducing impact, or accepting residual risk.

### 5.1 Treatment Options

| Option | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Avoid** | Eliminate the activity or condition that creates the risk. |
| **Reduce** | Implement controls that reduce likelihood or impact. |
| **Share** | Transfer risk through insurance, contracts, or delegation. |
| **Accept** | Explicitly acknowledge and retain the residual risk. |

### 5.2 Mitigation Strategy Schema

Each mitigation strategy is defined by:

| Field | Type | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `strategyId` | string | Unique identifier. |
| `description` | string | Strategy description. |
| `type` | string | `preventive`, `detective`, `corrective`, `compensating`. |
| `effectiveness` | string | `low`, `medium`, `high`. |
| `implementationStatus` | string | `planned`, `in_progress`, `implemented`, `failed`. |
| `resourcesRequired` | object | Personnel, budget, tools. |
| `targetCompletionDate` | string (ISO 8601) | Target completion. |

### 5.3 Treatment Approval

Treatment proposals must be approved through G-12 (Approval Manager):

1. Risk owner develops a treatment proposal.
2. Proposal is submitted to G-12 for approval.
3. Approval routes to the designated decision authority (based on risk severity and scope).
4. If approved, G-07 records the treatment and schedules implementation.
5. If rejected, risk owner develops an alternative proposal.

### 5.4 Treatment Monitoring

- G-07 tracks treatment implementation status.
- Treatment progress is reported to the Risk Committee quarterly.
- Failed treatments trigger risk re-assessment.
- Treatment completion triggers risk closure evaluation.

## 6. Risk Monitoring and Review

Risk monitoring is the continuous process of tracking risk status, treatment progress, and new risk emergence.

### 6.1 Continuous Monitoring

| Monitoring Activity | Frequency | Responsible |
|---------------------|-----------|-------------|
| Risk score recalculation | On trigger events | G-07 |
| Treatment status check | Weekly | G-07 |
| New risk identification | Continuous (event-driven) | All components via G-14 |
| Risk owner health check | Monthly | G-07 |
| Risk register review | Quarterly | Risk Committee |

### 6.2 Risk Review Triggers

Reviews are triggered by:

- **Scheduled**: Quarterly review by the Risk Committee.
- **Event-based**: Significant governance changes, policy updates, or new compliance requirements.
- **Incident-based**: Following a governance incident or near-miss.
- **Audit-based**: Following an audit finding or conformance failure.
- **Threshold-based**: When risk score crosses defined thresholds.

### 6.3 Monitoring Outputs

- Risk status change events.
- Treatment progress reports.
- New risk identification events.
- Risk posture aggregation (to G-08, G-15).

## 7. Risk Appetite

**Risk appetite** defines the acceptable level of risk that AI-OS is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives. Risk appetite parameters are set by the Governance Council through policy and charter.

### 7.1 Risk Appetite Statement

The overall risk appetite is defined in the Governance Charter (G-03):

| Category | Risk Appetite Level | Rationale |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Operational** | Medium | Governance operations should be stable but not overly constrained. |
| **Security** | Low | AI-OS handles sensitive data; security risks must be minimized. |
| **Compliance** | Very Low | Regulatory compliance is mandatory; violations must be rare. |
| **Reputational** | Low | AI-OS reputation depends on trustworthy governance. |
| **Strategic** | Medium | Some strategic risk is acceptable for innovation. |

### 7.2 Risk Appetite Parameters

These parameters operationalize the risk appetite as measurable risk thresholds, a residual risk cap, and a review cycle. They are set by the Governance Council and must not be exceeded by accepted residual risk.

| Parameter | Description | Classification |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| `maximumRiskScore` | Maximum aggregate risk score per domain. | Measurable risk threshold |
| `maximumLikelihood` | Maximum individual risk likelihood. | Measurable risk threshold |
| `maximumImpact` | Maximum individual risk impact. | Measurable risk threshold |
| `residualRiskCap` | Maximum acceptable residual risk after treatment. | Residual risk cap |
| `reviewPeriod` | Mandatory review cycle for each risk level. | Review cycle |

### 7.3 Risk Appetite Changes

Changes to risk appetite require:

1. Policy proposal through G-01.
2. Approval by the Governance Council via G-12.
3. Communication to all risk owners and components.
4. Re-assessment of existing risks against new risk appetite levels (and their thresholds).

## 8. Risk Authority and Accountability

### 8.1 Risk Authority Model

Risk authority follows the hierarchical authority model (see [13.1 §6](./13.1-Architecture-Overview.md#6-authority-model)):

| Level | Authority Source | Risk Responsibilities |
|-------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Supreme** | Organization leadership | Set overall risk appetite. |
| **System** | Governance framework, Risk Committee | Define risk appetite parameters, approve high-risk treatments. |
| **Domain** | Domain stewards | Manage risks within their domain, approve domain-level treatments. |
| **Operational** | Risk owners | Implement risk treatments, report on risk status. |
| **Emergency** | Incident commanders | Take emergency actions to mitigate critical risks. |

### 8.2 Risk Accountability

Every risk must have a designated **risk owner** who is accountable for:

- Risk identification within their scope.
- Risk assessment and scoring.
- Treatment plan development and execution.
- Regular status reporting.
- Risk closure recommendation.

Risk owners are recorded via the Authority Schema with `authorityType: expertise` or `authorityType: role_based`, and their actions are bound to principals via G-10.

### 8.3 Risk Escalation

| Risk Level | Escalation Path |
|------------|-----------------|
| Low | Risk owner monitors. |
| Medium | Report to domain risk lead. |
| High | Report to Risk Committee. |
| Very High | Report to Governance Council. |
| Critical | Emergency escalation to Supreme Authority. |

### 8.4 Risk Acceptance Authority (Bounded)

Formal acceptance of residual risk (the **Accept** treatment option, §5.1) is a bounded authority. Only the bodies below may issue `governance.risk.accepted`, and only within their stated ceiling. Acceptance must never exceed the risk appetite parameters defined in §7.

| Accepting Authority | Permitted Acceptance Ceiling | Constraint |
|---------------------|------------------------------|------------|
| **Risk Owner** | Low and Medium residual risk, within domain appetite parameters | May not accept risk that exceeds `residualRiskCap`. |
| **Risk Committee** | High residual risk | Requires recorded rationale and a monitoring plan; cannot accept Very High or Critical. |
| **Governance Council** | Very High residual risk | Requires documented approval via G-12 and a named review date. |
| **Supreme Authority** | Critical residual risk | Only by explicit, time-boxed exception; auto-expires per `reviewPeriod`. |

**Hard limits (non-delegable):**

- No authority may accept residual risk whose score exceeds the applicable `maximumRiskScore` or `residualRiskCap` from §7.2.
- Acceptance of Very High or Critical risk is prohibited by default and permitted only via the Governance Council (Very High) or Supreme Authority (Critical) ceilings above, each with a mandatory review.
- All acceptance decisions are sealed audit events (G-09) and bound to the accountable principal via G-10.

## 10. Compliance Governance Model

**Compliance governance** ensures AI-OS conforms to applicable regulations, standards, contractual obligations, and internal policies. It establishes the processes, roles, and evidence requirements for demonstrating compliance.

### 10.1 Compliance Governance Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Obligation-Centric** | Compliance is driven by specific obligations, each with evidence and review requirements. |
| **Evidence-Based** | Compliance assertions require verifiable evidence, not self-attestation alone. |
| **Continuous Validation** | Compliance is continuously assessed, not point-in-time. |
| **Traceable** | Every compliance assertion traces to its source obligation and supporting evidence. |
| **Auditable** | All compliance activities produce immutable audit records. |

### 10.2 Compliance Governance Scope

Compliance governance covers:

- **Regulatory compliance**: GDPR, CCPA, SOX, HIPAA (as applicable).
- **Standards compliance**: ISO 27001, ISO 20000, SOC 2, NIST CSF.
- **Contractual compliance**: SLAs, customer agreements.
- **Internal policy compliance**: AI-OS governance policies and standards.

## 11. Compliance Baseline

The **compliance baseline** is the authoritative set of compliance obligations and controls that the AI-OS system must satisfy. It is maintained by G-08 (Compliance Manager) and stored as an artifact in G-03.

### 11.1 Baseline Definition

The baseline includes:

1. **Obligation register**: All applicable compliance obligations.
2. **Control baseline**: All required controls with their design and operating status.
3. **Evidence requirements**: What evidence is needed for each obligation/control.
4. **Review schedule**: When each obligation/control is reviewed.

### 11.2 Baseline Versioning

- The baseline is versioned (semantic versioning).
- Each version has an effective date.
- Baseline changes require approval through G-12.
- Previous baseline versions are retained for audit purposes.

### 11.3 Baseline Distribution

- The baseline is published to G-03 as a snapshot.
- G-15 (Conformance Manager) consumes the baseline for conformance evaluation.
- G-13 (Control Manager) consumes the baseline for control design.
- G-09 (Audit Manager) uses the baseline for audit planning.

## 12. Obligation Register

The **obligation register** is the comprehensive inventory of all compliance obligations applicable to AI-OS. It is maintained by G-08.

### 12.1 Obligation Entry

Compliance obligations are **externally derived mandates** — they originate from regulations, standards, contracts, or internal policy that AI-OS must conform to (see §10.2). They are categorically distinct from **internal risk treatment** (§5), which is the organization's own choice of how to modify its risk posture. An obligation is satisfied by evidence of conformance; a risk treatment is an action that reduces or accepts risk. A single obligation may require several controls (§14), and the residual risk of meeting (or failing) that obligation remains a separate, risk-tracked item. This separation ensures a compliance gap is never silently reclassified as an accepted risk, nor vice versa.

Each obligation is defined by:

| Field | Type | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `obligationId` | string | Unique identifier. |
| `name` | string | Human-readable name. |
| `description` | string | Detailed description of the obligation. |
| `source` | string | Regulatory standard, contract, or policy source. |
| `scope` | string | Applicability scope (global, system, domain). |
| `category` | string | Obligation category (data protection, security, etc.). |
| `severity` | string | `low`, `medium`, `high`, `critical`. |
| `controlMapping` | array[string] | IDs of controls that satisfy this obligation. |
| `evidenceRequirements` | array[EvidenceRequirement] | What evidence is needed. |
| `reviewPeriod` | string | Review frequency (e.g., "quarterly"). |
| `owner` | string | Obligation owner (principal). |
| `effectiveFrom` | string (ISO 8601) | When the obligation becomes effective. |
| `effectiveUntil` | string (ISO 8601) or null | When the obligation expires. |

### 12.2 Evidence Requirements

Each obligation specifies evidence requirements:

| Field | Type | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `evidenceType` | string | Type of evidence (report, log, attestation, test result). |
| `frequency` | string | How often evidence must be produced. |
| `stalenessLimit` | string | Maximum age of acceptable evidence. |
| `verificationMethod` | string | How evidence is verified (hash chain, third-party, etc.). |

### 12.3 Obligation States

```text
Obligation states:
IDENTIFIED → IMPORTED → VALIDATED → ACTIVE → MONITORING → EVIDENT → REVIEW_DUE
                                                 → EVIDENCE_STALE → RENEWAL_REQUIRED
                            ↘ SUPERSEDED (from re-import)
```

## 13. Compliance Monitoring

Compliance monitoring is the continuous process of assessing whether the AI-OS system meets its compliance obligations and produces the required evidence.

### 13.1 Monitoring Activities

| Activity | Frequency | Responsible |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Obligation status check | Daily | G-08 |
| Evidence currency check | Daily | G-08 |
| Control test result review | As tests complete | G-13, G-08 |
| Compliance gap analysis | Quarterly | G-08, G-15 |
| External compliance reporting | Per framework schedule | G-08 |
| Stakeholder review | Quarterly | Compliance Committee |

### 13.2 Compliance Gap Detection

Gaps are detected through:

1. **Evidence staleness**: Evidence has exceeded its staleness limit.
2. **Control failure**: A control test result is failed or partial.
3. **Obligation expiration**: An obligation's `effectiveUntil` has passed without renewal.
4. **Conformance failure**: G-15 identifies a compliance conformance gap.
5. **Audit finding**: G-09 audit identifies a compliance issue.

### 13.3 Gap Response

When a compliance gap is detected:

1. G-08 flags the gap as a compliance finding.
2. The finding is routed to G-09 for audit record.
3. The finding triggers a risk identification (via G-07) if severity warrants.
4. The finding is reported to the Compliance Committee.
5. A remediation plan is developed and approved through G-12.

## 14. Control-Governance Linkage

Controls are the mechanisms that ensure obligations are met. The **control-governance linkage** ensures every obligation maps to at least one control, and every control traces to at least one obligation.

### 14.1 Control-Obligation Mapping

```text
Obligation → Controls → Evidence → Compliance Assertion → Conformance Score
```

| Mapping Relationship | Description |
|----------------------|-------------|
| **Obligation → Control** | Each obligation maps to one or more controls. |
| **Control → Evidence** | Each control produces evidence of its operation. |
| **Evidence → Assertion** | Evidence is aggregated into compliance assertions. |
| **Assertion → Score** | Assertions contribute to overall compliance scores. |

### 14.2 Control Governance

Controls are governed by G-13 (Control Manager) in coordination with G-08:

- **Control design**: G-08 specifies obligation requirements; G-13 designs controls to meet them.
- **Control implementation**: Operating context implements controls; G-13 records implementation.
- **Control testing**: Controls are tested per schedule; G-13 records results.
- **Control effectiveness**: G-13 assesses and reports control effectiveness.
- **Control remediation**: Failed controls trigger remediation via G-12.

### 14.3 Control Lifecycle Integration

The control lifecycle (see [components.md §5.13](./components.md)) integrates with compliance governance:

```text
Obligation Identified → Control Designed → Control Implemented → Control Tested → 
  Effectiveness Assessed → Compliance Assertion → Conformance Score
```

## 17. Risk-Compliance Integration

Risk and compliance governance are tightly integrated:

### 17.1 Risk from Compliance

Compliance violations and gaps are treated as risks:

1. A compliance violation is detected (`governance.compliance.violation.detected`).
2. G-08 assesses the risk level of the violation.
3. If severity ≥ Medium, G-07 creates a risk entry.
4. The risk is treated as a compliance risk with appropriate owner.

### 17.2 Compliance from Risk

High-severity risks that could lead to compliance violations trigger:

1. G-07 identifies the risk could cause a compliance breach.
2. G-07 escalates to the Risk Committee.
3. If compliance impact is likely, G-07 notifies G-08.
4. G-08 adds the risk as a proactive compliance monitoring item.

### 17.3 Shared Evidence

Risk and compliance share evidence:

- Risk mitigation strategies produce evidence used for compliance.
- Compliance evidence (control tests, audit results) informs risk likelihood/impact.
- Both trace to the same governance artifacts in G-03 and audit records in G-09.

Where this document uses "threshold" language (§8.4, §9.1 escalation, §7.2 parameter classification), it refers to the current `Risk Threshold` concept, consistent with the glossary — not to the deprecated `Risk Tolerance`.
