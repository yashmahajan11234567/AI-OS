## 1. Governance Body Architecture

**Governance bodies** (councils and committees) are the collective decision-making organs of the AI-OS governance architecture. They operate at the intersection of authority, policy, and oversight — translating organizational intent into concrete governance decisions, policies, and directives.

### 1.1 Governance Body Model

```text
Supreme Authority (Org Leadership)
    ↓
System Authority
    ↓
Domain Authority
    ↓
  ┌──────────────┐
  │ Governance   │
  │ Council (G-04)│ ← Collective decision-making body
  └──────┬──────┘
         │
    ┌────┴────┐
    ▼         ▼
Committee   Committee   Committee
  (1)         (2)         (N)
```

Governance bodies are **collective authorities** — they do not act as individuals but as assemblies with defined quorum, voting, and decision rules. Their authority is recorded via the Authority Schema (see [§5](#5-authority-types) and [schemas.md §Authority Schema](./schemas.md)) with `authorityType: organizational` or `authorityType: role_based`.

### 1.2 Core Properties

Each governance body is defined by:

- **Charter** (stored in G-03): Scope, responsibilities, authority limits, decision rights, and membership rules.
- **Quorum rules**: Minimum participation for valid decisions.
- **Voting rules**: Decision thresholds (simple majority, supermajority, unanimity).
- **Membership**: Appointed principals with explicit authority grants (G-05).
- **Scope**: System-wide, domain-specific, or cross-domain.
- **Temporal bounds**: Effective dates and review cycles.

## 2. Authority Model for Governance Bodies

Governance bodies derive their authority from the hierarchical authority model defined in [13.1 §6](./13.1-Architecture-Overview.md#6-authority-model). The authority model provides the structural framework:

### Authority Hierarchy for Governance Bodies

| Level | Source | Governance Body Role |
|-------|--------|---------------------|
| **Supreme Authority** | Organization leadership | Establishes the charter for the Governance Council; defines scope for system-level authorities. |
| **System Authority** | Governance framework, Security Council, Validation Council | Delegates authority to domain-level committees (Security, Compliance, Audit councils). |
| **Domain Authority** | Domain stewards, Policy Engineers | Appoints members to domain-specific committees (Agent Governance, Data Governance, etc.). |
| **Operational Authority** | Individual agents, workflows | Not applicable as a source — governance bodies govern operational authority, they do not derive from it. |

### Authority Principles for Governance Bodies

1. **Explicit Charter**: Every governance body MUST have a charter artifact registered in G-03.
2. **Scoped Authority**: Authority is bounded by the body's charter scope and limitations.
3. **Accountability**: Governance body decisions are attributed to the body (via the collective authority), not to individual members (unless explicitly recorded as individual votes).
4. **Immutability**: Charter changes, membership changes, and decision records are immutable events in G-09.
5. **Emergency Override**: Emergency authority (via designated emergency responders) can override normal governance body decisions for critical situations.

## 3. Governance Council (G-04)

The **Governance Council** is the principal collective decision-making body in the AI-OS governance architecture. Implemented as component G-04, it provides the interface for formal governance deliberation and decision publication.

### 3.1 Purpose

> *"G-04 Governance Council — Formal interface to the governance body: define charter, register committees, appoint members, define scope and authority, and publish governance outcomes from council proceedings."*

(from [components.md §5.5](./components.md))

### 3.2 Responsibilities

- Maintain governance body charter (in G-03 as artifact).
- Register standing committees and ad-hoc committees with scope and authority limits.
- Record appointment and revocation of council and committee members.
- Publish council decisions as policy proposals (routed to G-01) or as direct approvals (routed to G-12).
- Maintain quorum and voting rules per committee.
- Produce governance minutes and decision records (to G-09).

### 3.2 Non-Responsibilities

- Does not execute policy evaluation — delegates to G-02.
- Does not approve day-to-day operational exceptions — delegates to G-11 and appropriate committee.
- Does not enforce decisions — produces decisions; enforcement is caller responsibility.

### 3.3 Inputs

- Council decision (policies, exceptions, charters)
- Committee formation / dissolution requests
- Appointment revocation requests
- Voting outcome

### 3.4 Outputs

- Approved/Rejected decision records
- Policy proposals (to G-01)
- Direct approvals (to G-12)
- Charter changes (as artifact to G-03)
- Governance minutes (to G-09)

### 3.5 Interfaces

- **Inbound:** Council decision events; committee lifecycle events.
- **Outbound:** `registerCharter(charter) → G-03`; `proposePolicy(proposal) → G-01`; `approveDecision(decision) → G-12`; `emit(event) → G-14`; `recordMinutes(minutes) → G-09`
- **Query:** `getCharter(version)`; `getCommittee(name)`; `listMembers(committee)`

### 3.6 Authority

- Owns charter and committee definitions.
- Owns appointment/removal of council members (subject to allocation — see Part 5).
- Cannot unilaterally override published policy — amendment requires publication flow via G-01.

### 3.7 State Model

```text
Council:
ACTIVE → SUSPENDED (requires replacement authority) → REACTIVATED

Committee:
PROPOSED → APPROVED → ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → DISSOLVED
```

### 3.8 Security Boundary

Council minutes and internal deliberations are restricted to members and designated observers. Published outcomes are world-readable. Appointment records are sensitive (PII-like for council members).

### 3.9 Failure Boundary

Council dissolution without succession = governance halt. Cannot enter suspended state without replacement authority registered in G-03. Failure to produce minutes is a compliance violation (G-08) and audit finding (G-09).

## 4. Committee Types

Governance committees are specialized bodies established under the council's charter to address specific governance concerns. Committees inherit their authority from the council via delegation.

### 4.1 Standing Committees

Standing committees are permanent bodies with ongoing governance responsibilities:

| Committee | Domain | Scope | Authority Source |
|-----------|--------|-------|------------------|
| **Policy Committee** | Policy Governance | System-wide and domain-specific policy | Domain Authority → System Authority |
| **Security Council** | Security Governance | Cross-domain security policies, incident response | System Authority |
| **Validation Council** | Conformance Governance | Conformance standards, validation criteria | System Authority |
| **Compliance Committee** | Compliance Governance | Regulatory and standards compliance | System Authority → Domain Authority |
| **Audit Committee** | Audit Governance | Audit scope, methodology, findings oversight | System Authority |
| **Agent Governance Committee** | Agent Governance | Agent identity, capabilities, lifecycle | Domain Authority |
| **Data Governance Committee** | Data Governance | Data classification, lineage, access, retention | Domain Authority |
| **Risk Committee** | Risk Governance | Risk identification, assessment, treatment | Domain Authority |

### 4.2 Ad-Hoc Committees

Ad-hoc committees are formed for specific, time-bound governance activities:

- **Incident Response Committee**: Formed during security incidents or emergency situations.
- **Architecture Review Board**: Formed for major architecture changes or new capability reviews.
- **Special Investigation Committee**: Formed for targeted governance reviews or disputes.

### 4.3 Committee Charter Elements

Each committee charter includes:

1. **Scope definition**: Precise boundaries of the committee's authority.
2. **Responsibility list**: Specific duties and deliverables.
3. **Membership rules**: Selection criteria, appointment process, term lengths.
4. **Decision rules**: Voting thresholds, quorum requirements.
5. **Delegation limits**: Maximum scope and level of authority that can be delegated further.
6. **Reporting structure**: To whom the committee reports and with what frequency.
7. **Review cycle**: How often charter and scope are reviewed.

## 5. Membership Management

### 5.1 Authority Types

Members of governance bodies hold authority via the following types (see also [schemas.md §Authority Schema](./schemas.md)):

| Type | Description | Governance Body Use |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| `role_based` | Derived from a role assignment (Part 5). | Standard council/committee membership. |
| `delegated` | Transferred from another authority via delegation. | Deputy members acting on behalf of principals. |
| `expertise` | Based on domain expertise certification. | Subject matter experts appointed for specific reviews. |
| `organizational` | Derived from organizational position. | Executive members with positional authority. |
| `derived` | Computed from a combination of other authorities. | Composite membership for cross-functional decisions. |

### 5.2 Membership Appointment

Membership appointments follow this process:

1. **Nomination**: A member is nominated by an authority holder (typically the appointing body).
2. **Approval**: The nomination is routed through G-12 for approval if required by charter.
3. **Authority Grant**: G-05 records the authority grant for the member's scope and responsibilities.
4. **Registration**: G-03 registers the member as part of the committee roster.
5. **Event Emission**: An appointment event is emitted through G-14.

### 5.3 Membership Lifecycle

```text
Member states:
NOMINATED → APPROVED → ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → REVOKED → ARCHIVED
                    ↘ REJECTED (terminal)
```

- **NOMINATED**: Nomination submitted, awaiting approval.
- **APPROVED**: Nomination accepted, authority grant pending.
- **ACTIVE**: Member is fully operational with authority.
- **SUSPENDED**: Membership temporarily paused (e.g., during investigation).
- **REVOKED**: Membership permanently removed.
- **ARCHIVED**: Retention-only state.

### 5.4 Membership Constraints

- Members must be valid principals (Part 1, Part 5).
- Terms are time-bounded and must be renewed.
- Members cannot exceed their authority scope.
- Members must have appropriate accountability bindings (G-10).

## 6. Charter and Scope

### 6.1 Charter Definition

A **charter** is the formal document that establishes a governance body's existence, scope, authority, and operating procedures. Charters are stored as immutable artifacts in G-03 and are subject to the policy lifecycle (see [13.3 Policy Architecture](./13.3-Policy-Architecture.md)).

### 6.2 Charter Elements

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Name** | Official designation of the body. |
| **Purpose** | Why the body exists. |
| **Scope** | Boundaries of authority (subject, resource, action, environment, temporal). |
| **Responsibilities** | Specific duties and deliverables. |
| **Membership Rules** | Selection criteria, appointment process, term lengths, quorum requirements. |
| **Decision Rules** | Voting thresholds, quorum requirements, tie-breaking procedures. |
| **Delegation Authority** | Maximum scope and level of authority that can be delegated. |
| **Reporting Structure** | To whom the body reports and with what frequency. |
| **Review Cycle** | How often the charter is reviewed and by whom. |
| **Effective Dates** | When the charter takes effect and when it expires. |

### 6.3 Scope Dimensions

Each charter defines scope through multiple dimensions:

| Dimension | Description | Constraint |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **Subject Scope** | Which actors, agents, or principals the body governs. | Must align with Part 5 principal allocation. |
| **Resource Scope** | Which resources, systems, or data the body's authority covers. | Must reference entities registered in G-03. |
| **Action Scope** | Which types of decisions the body can make. | Must be a subset of the parent authority's actions. |
| **Environment Scope** | Deployment environment, region, trust zone. | Must not exceed domain environment bounds. |
| **Temporal Scope** | Valid from / until timestamps. | Must be explicitly bounded. |

### 6.4 Charter Lifecycle

```text
Charter states:
DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED → ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → TERMINATED
                 ↘ REJECTED (terminal)
```

All charter changes require approval through G-12 and are recorded as immutable events in G-09.

## 7. Decision-Making Authority

### 7.1 Decision Rights

Governance bodies hold decision rights that are explicitly granted and recorded:

- **Policy creation/modification**: Authority to propose, modify, and approve governance policies.
- **Exception granting**: Authority to approve or deny policy exceptions.
- **Authority delegation**: Authority to delegate decision rights to committee members or other bodies.
- **Charter approval**: Authority to approve charter changes for themselves and subordinate committees.
- **Compliance determination**: Authority to make compliance determinations on obligations.

### 7.2 Decision Types

| Type | Description | Authority Level | Approval Required |
|------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Policy Decision** | Approves/denies policy creation, modification, retirement. | Domain/System | G-12 |
| **Exception Decision** | Grants/denies exceptions to policy. | Operational/Domain | G-12 |
| **Authority Decision** | Delegates or revokes authority. | Operational and above | G-12 |
| **Compliance Decision** | Determines compliance status on obligations. | Domain/System | G-12 |
| **Risk Decision** | Accepts or treats identified risks. | Domain/System | G-12 |
| **Charter Decision** | Approves charter changes. | Same body or parent | G-12 |

### 7.3 Decision Process

The governance decision process:

1. **Decision Trigger**: An event or scheduled review triggers a decision need.
2. **Agenda Setting**: The council/committee chair sets the agenda.
3. **Deliberation**: Members discuss the decision item.
4. **Voting**: Members vote per the committee's voting rules.
5. **Decision Recording**: The outcome is recorded as a `GovernanceDecision` artifact in G-03.
6. **Approval Routing**: If required, the decision is routed through G-12.
7. **Event Emission**: Decision events (`governance.decision.*`) are emitted via G-14.
8. **Implementation**: The decision is enacted through the appropriate component.

### 7.4 Decision Schema

Decisions are recorded using the Decision Schema (see [schemas.md §Decision Schema](./schemas.md)):

| Field | Type | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `decisionId` | string (UUID v4) | Unique identifier. |
| `name` | string | Human-readable name. |
| `version` | string | Semantic version. |
| `description` | string | Decision context and outcome. |
| `decisionType` | string | Type: `policy_approval`, `exception_grant`, `override_authorization`, `risk_acceptance`, `compliance_determination`, `audit_conclusion`. |
| `policyReferences` | array[PolicyReference] | Policies evaluated. |
| `subject` | string | Entity the decision pertains to. |
| `subjectType` | string | Type of subject: `agent`, `workflow`, `resource`, `data`, `knowledge`. |
| `outcome` | string | Result: `approved`, `denied`, `conditionally_approved`, `requires_review`, `escalated`. |
| `reasoning` | string | Explanation of reasoning. |
| `authorities` | array[AuthorityReference] | Governing authorities that participated. |
| `effectiveFrom` | string (ISO 8601) | Effectiveness start. |
| `effectiveUntil` | string (ISO 8601) or null | Expiry (if applicable). |

## 8. Delegation to Governance Bodies

### 8.1 Delegation Architecture

Governance bodies receive their authority through the delegation architecture (see [13.4 Decision Authority and Delegation](./13.4-Decision-Authority-and-Delegation.md)). The delegation process:

1. The Supreme or System Authority grants a charter to the Governance Council.
2. The Council's charter delegates authority to standing and ad-hoc committees.
3. Committees delegate specific decision rights to members as authority grants.

### 8.2 Delegation Schema

Committee delegations use the Delegation Schema (see [schemas.md §Delegation Schema](./schemas.md)):

| Field | Type | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `delegationId` | string (UUID v4) | Unique identifier. |
| `name` | string | Human-readable name. |
| `version` | string | Semantic version. |
| `description` | string | Purpose and scope. |
| `delegatorId` | string | Entity delegating (`authority`, `governance_body`, `role`). |
| `delegatorType` | string | Type: `agent`, `authority`, `governance_body`, `role`. |
| `delegateeId` | string | Entity receiving authority. |
| `delegateeType` | string | Type: `agent`, `authority`, `governance_body`, `role`. |
| `authorityId` | string (UUID v4) | Specific authority being delegated. |
| `scope` | string | Applicability: `global`, `system`, `workflow`, `agent_group`, `agent`. |
| `limitations` | array[string] | Constraints on the delegation. |
| `effectiveFrom` | string (ISO 8601) | Start of validity. |
| `effectiveUntil` | string (ISO 8601) or null | End of validity. |

### 8.3 Delegation Validation

Every delegation to a governance body MUST be validated:

- **Authority bound**: The `authorityId` must reference a valid, active authority.
- **Scope fidelity**: The delegation scope must not exceed the delegator's authority scope.
- **Temporal bounds**: `effectiveFrom` ≥ delegator's authority `effectiveFrom`; `effectiveUntil` ≤ delegator's authority `effectiveUntil`.
- **No self-delegation**: `delegatorId` ≠ `delegateeId`.
- **No circular delegation**: The delegation graph must be acyclic (enforced at grant creation by G-06).

### 8.4 Delegation Depth

Delegation chains to and from governance bodies are subject to depth constraints:

- Maximum delegation depth is configurable in charter (default: 4).
- Each delegation specifies how much further it may be delegated (`maxDelegationDepth`).
- `maxDelegationDepth` ≥ `delegationDepth` + 1 for valid delegations.

## 9. Meetings and Proceedings

### 9.1 Meeting Types

| Meeting Type | Purpose | Frequency | Participants |
|-------------|---------|-----------|--------------|
| **Council Sitting** | Regular governance decisions, policy reviews. | Scheduled (weekly/monthly/quarterly). | Council members, invited experts. |
| **Committee Meeting** | Domain-specific reviews, issue resolution. | Scheduled per committee charter. | Committee members, relevant stakeholders. |
| **Special Session** | Emergency or urgent matters. | On-demand. | Relevant members only. |
| **Joint Session** | Multi-committee coordination. | As needed. | Members of participating committees. |

### 9.2 Meeting Proceedings

Meeting proceedings are recorded as follows:

- **Agenda**: Published in advance with correlation ID.
- **Minutes**: Record of deliberations, votes, and decisions.
- **Attendance**: Recorded with principal identity (G-10).
- **Decisions**: Recorded via Decision Schema in G-03.
- **Actions**: Assigned follow-up items with owners and deadlines.

All minutes and decisions are routed to G-09 for permanent audit storage.

### 9.3 Meeting Security

- Council minutes and internal deliberations are restricted to members and designated observers.
- Published outcomes (decisions, policies) are world-readable.
- Appointment records are sensitive (PII-like for council members).
- Meeting recordings, if made, follow the same classification as minutes.

## 10. Quorum and Voting

### 10.1 Quorum Rules

**Quorum** is the minimum number of members who must participate for a meeting or decision to be valid.

| Body Type | Quorum Requirement | Special Provisions |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Governance Council** | Majority of appointed members. | Alternate members may substitute for absent primary members. |
| **Standing Committee** | As defined in charter (typically 3+ members). | Charter may specify fractional quorum (e.g., 50% + 1). |
| **Ad-Hoc Committee** | All appointed members (or as defined in formation charter). | Quorum requirements stated in the formation charter. |
| **Emergency Session** | As defined in emergency procedure. | May allow remote participation via secure channels. |

### 10.2 Voting Rules

Voting rules define how decisions are made once quorum is achieved:

| Rule Type | Description | Required For |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Simple Majority** | More than half of votes cast. | Routine policy decisions, exception approvals. |
| **Supermajority** | 2/3 or 3/4 of votes cast. | Charter changes, major policy modifications, authority delegation. |
| **Unanimity** | All eligible members agree. | Emergency authority invocation, fundamental governance changes. |
| **Consensus** | No sustained objections (modified consensus). | High-impact decisions, architectural changes. |

### 10.3 Voting Record

Every vote is recorded with:

- Voter principal identity (G-10).
- Vote value (`approve`, `deny`, `abstain`, `condition`).
- Timestamp.
- Reasoning (if applicable).
- All votes are immutable and auditable.

## 11. Council State Model

The Governance Council follows this state model:

```text
Council:
ACTIVE → SUSPENDED (requires replacement authority) → REACTIVATED

Committee:
PROPOSED → APPROVED → ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → DISSOLVED
```

### 11.1 Council States

| State | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **ACTIVE** | The council is fully operational and can make decisions. |
| **SUSPENDED** | The council is temporarily non-operational (e.g., during investigation). Can only transition to this state if replacement authority is registered in G-03. |
| **REACTIVATED** | The council resumes operations after suspension. |

### 11.2 Committee States

| State | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **PROPOSED** | Committee formation proposed; awaiting approval. |
| **APPROVED** | Committee approved but not yet active. |
| **ACTIVE** | Committee is fully operational. |
| **SUSPENDED** | Committee temporarily paused. |
| **DISSOLVED** | Committee permanently terminated. |

## 12. Governance Body Events

Governance body events are registered in the `governance.authority.*` event namespace (see [governance-events.md §3.3](./governance-events.md#33-authority-aggregate)) and the `governance.decision.*` namespace (see [§3.2](./governance-events.md#32-decision-aggregate)).

### 12.1 Authority Events

| Event | Trigger |
|-------|---------|
| `governance.authority.delegated` | A delegation grant is created (e.g., committee charter approved, member appointment). |
| `governance.authority.revoked` | An authority or delegation is revoked (e.g., member removed, committee dissolved). |

> **Note:** The Authority Aggregate (see [governance-events.md §3.3](./governance-events.md#33-authority-aggregate)) defines exactly two canonical events — `governance.authority.delegated` and `governance.authority.revoked`. Authority grant activation and temporary suspension are recorded through these events plus the governance decision/approval flow (`governance.decision.*`, `governance.approval.*`) rather than as distinct authority-namespace events.

### 12.2 Decision Events

| Event | Trigger |
|-------|---------|
| `governance.decision.created` | A governance decision record was opened. |
| `governance.decision.approved` | A governance decision was authorized by required holders. |
| `governance.decision.rejected` | A governance decision was denied by required holders. |

### 12.3 Approval Events

| Event | Trigger |
|-------|---------|
| `governance.approval.requested` | A formal request for approval was submitted (e.g., policy proposal, charter change). |
| `governance.approval.granted` | An approval request was granted. |
| `governance.approval.rejected` | An approval request was denied. |

### 12.4 Council-Specific Events

| Event | Trigger |
|-------|---------|
| `CouncilSitting` | A council sitting was convened. |
| `DecisionPublished` | A council decision was published. |
| `CommitteeFormed` | A committee was formed. |
| `CommitteeDissolved` | A committee was dissolved. |
| `MemberAppointed` | A member was appointed to a council or committee. |
| `MemberRevoked` | A member's appointment was revoked. |
| `QuorumNotMet` | Quorum was not achieved for a meeting. |

## 13. Integration with Governance Components

The governance body architecture integrates with Part 13 governance components as follows:

| Component | Governance Body Integration |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **G-00 Governance Manager** | Orchestrates governance sessions involving council decisions. Records session context for accountability. |
| **G-01 Policy Manager** | Receives policy proposals from the council. Records approved policies as policy packages. |

| **G-09 Audit Manager** | Records all council minutes, member appointments, charter changes, and decision records as immutable audit entries. |
| **G-10 Accountability Manager** | Binds all council actions to accountable principals (members, the council as a collective). |
| **G-11 Exception Manager** | Routes exception requests that require council-level review. |
| **G-12 Approval Manager** | Routes council decisions requiring approval, records approval outcomes. |
| **G-14 Governance Event Manager** | Routes all council and committee events. |
| **G-15 Conformance Manager** | Evaluates whether governance body processes conform to charter and policy. |

## 14. Cross-Part Integration

Governance bodies integrate across AI-OS parts:

| Part | Integration |
|------|-------------|
| **Part 1** | Principal identity model — members must be valid principals; G-10 binds actions to principals. |
| **Part 5** | Principal allocation model for member appointment — G-04 appointments reference Part 5 allocations. |
| **Part 6** | Committee scope definitions — G-04 registerCommittee creates committees matching Part 6 scope definitions. |
| **Part 12** | Audit model — minutes, decisions, and membership records are audit artifacts (G-09 implements Part 12 audit contract). |

### 14.1 Bootstrap Governance Body

During system bootstrap, governance bodies are initialized through a declared root principal (platform authority) with artifact backing in G-03. This root authority provisions:

1. The G-04 Governance Council charter.
2. Initial standing committee charters (Security, Compliance, Audit).
3. Seed membership appointments.
4. Initial authority grants for council and committee chairs.

No delegation occurs during bootstrap — all initial grants are direct authority grants via G-05.

### 14.2 Part 5 Principal Allocation

Part 5 defines the hierarchy of principals and groups. G-04 (Governance Council) is the component realizing Part 6 committee definitions. G-05 (Decision Authority Manager) is the runtime expression of authority allocations — it does not create principal relationships, it enforces them. Authority grants for council and committee members reference Part 5 principals via G-10 principal binding.
